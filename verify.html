The checks that carry the teaching

Four of these are load-bearing, in the sense that if they fail then a page on this site is teaching something false.

  • The inverse transform is exact. cdf(invCdf(p)) − p is asserted below 1e-12 with no sampling involved. Because it is deterministic, a failure is a real bug rather than an unlucky run.
  • Ratio quantiles. For a fixed displacement over a Gaussian age the quantiles have a closed form, D / (µ + σΦ⁻¹(1−p)). This underwrites the whole of Method 1.
  • The monotonicity acceptance rate. Φ((µ₂−µ₁)/√(σ₁²+σ₂²)), checked at three separations including the one used on the mispairing page.
  • The open-interval bias. 1 − t_MRE/t_m, checked against Monte Carlo medians on four markers.

The remaining checks cover the random number generator, the analytic moments of every distribution shape, seed reproducibility, and the ability of the incremental estimator to recover the slip history its test data were built from.

Monte Carlo slip rates, a teaching site by James La Greca, University of Melbourne. The engine is checked against closed-form results on the verify page. Method provenance and reading are on the references page.

Every teaching dataset on this site is synthetic. Two presets carry digitised bracket values from published studies and are labelled as such. No unpublished field measurement appears anywhere in this repository.
