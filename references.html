Data statement

Read this before reusing anything

The teaching datasets on this site are synthetic. They were invented to isolate one teaching point each, and most are forward-modelled from a stated slip rate so that an estimator can be checked against the answer it should recover. They are not field sites. The marker labels M1 to M4 do not correspond to anything real.

Two presets are different, and are labelled published brackets in the interface. Those carry displacement and age bracket values from published studies. See the section below for what they are and what the recomputed numbers on this site do and do not represent.

No unpublished field measurement appears anywhere in this repository.

Method provenance

Incremental slip rates by rejection sampling

The accept-and-reject scheme on the incremental page is not original here. It builds on Gold and Cowgill (2011) and was developed in the form used on this site by Robert Zinke and colleagues. The JavaScript engine is a port of the algorithm implemented in Zinke's RISeR toolbox, and was checked against it.

  • Gold, R. D. and Cowgill, E. (2011). Earth and Planetary Science Letters. Cited by the RISeR documentation as the source of the method this code builds on. Full title, volume and DOI should be completed from the paper before this page is used as a citation source.
  • Zinke, R., Dolan, J. F., Rhodes, E. J., Van Dissen, R. and McGuire, C. P. (2017). Highly Variable Latest Pleistocene-Holocene Incremental Slip Rates on the Awatere Fault at Saxton River, South Island, New Zealand, Revealed by Lidar Mapping and Luminescence Dating. Geophysical Research Letters 44. doi:10.1002/2017GL075048
  • Zinke, R., Dolan, J. F., Rhodes, E. J., Van Dissen, R., McGuire, C. P., Hatem, A. E., Brown, N. D. and Langridge, R. M. (2019). Multimillennial Incremental Slip Rate Variability of the Clarence Fault at the Tophouse Road Site, Marlborough Fault System, New Zealand. Geophysical Research Letters 46. doi:10.1029/2018GL080688
  • Zinke, R. and others (2021). Latest Pleistocene to Holocene Incremental Slip Rates of the Wairau Fault: Implications for Long-Distance and Long-Term Coordination of Faulting Between North and South Island, New Zealand. Tectonics.

RISeR, the Rejection sampling for Incremental Slip Rate calculator, is by Robert Zinke (2019 to 2021). Its documentation asks that anyone using it cite Zinke and others (2017, 2019). This site is an independent reimplementation for teaching rather than a redistribution, but the intellectual debt is the same and the citation request applies just as much.

RISeR also computes a single-marker slip rate by dividing one probability density by another using a weighted convolution, following Bird (2007, equations A7 and A8), rather than by sampling. That is an exact alternative to the Monte Carlo route taken on the Method 1 page and it is worth knowing about.

Onset of offset

  • Hatem, A. E. and others. How Does the Onset of Offset Influence Geologic Slip Rates? This is the source for the anchoring correction used on the onset of offset page. Cited variously as 2024 and 2025 in the literature, which is usually an online-first against issue-date difference. Check the year against the version you have before citing it.

Earthquake-cycle variability in measured rates

  • Styron, R. (2019). Solid Earth 10, 15 to 25. On how earthquake cycle variability affects neotectonic and paleoseismic slip rate estimates. Relevant background for why a rate depends on the window it was measured over, a topic this site deliberately does not cover.

The two published presets

Both arrive as minimum and maximum brackets on displacement and age, digitised from the source studies, and are loaded here as uniform distributions because that is the shape a bracketing constraint implies.

What the recomputed numbers are not

The published analyses used fuller probability density shapes than the uniform brackets used here, and made site-specific choices this site does not reproduce. The rates this site computes from those presets are illustrative recomputations, not reproductions of the published results. Do not cite a number from this website as though it came from those papers. Go to the papers.

How to put real data on this site

The preset format is documented in docs/DATA-FORMAT.md in the repository. Replace the markers array and nothing else changes. An age can be given as a distribution specification, or as an empirical table of value and probability pairs, which is the same two-column format RISeR's makePDF.py writes. Calibrated ages exported from OxCal and converted with RISeR's calyr2age.py load without modification.

Verification

The engine is checked against closed-form results rather than against a second numerical library. The four load-bearing checks and their measured agreement are listed on the verify page, which runs them live. In a terminal the same checks run with node tools/verify.js.

About

Built by James La Greca, School of Geography, Earth and Atmospheric Sciences, University of Melbourne, as methods training material for postgraduate and early-career researchers working on geologic slip rates.

The code is a from-scratch JavaScript implementation with no dependencies and no build step. Every file served is a file in the repository, so the source you read is the source that ran.

Monte Carlo slip rates, a teaching site by James La Greca, University of Melbourne. The engine is checked against closed-form results on the verify page. Method provenance and reading are on the references page.
